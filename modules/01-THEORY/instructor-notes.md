# 01-THEORY — Instructor Notes (do not distribute to learners)

## Check your understanding — answers

1. Voltage: E or V, volt (V). Current: I, ampere (A). Resistance: R, ohm (Ω). Power: P, watt (W).
2. I = 24 ÷ 8 = **3 A**.
3. The meter injects its own small current to measure resistance; external voltage corrupts the reading and can damage the meter.

## Exercise 1 — key points

The receptacle supplies a fixed voltage (~120 V). The load's resistance determines the current (I = E ÷ R). The 20 A breaker is a maximum limit, not what is delivered.

## Exercise 3 — notes

Expected ≈ 10 mA. Check every meter is in series before power-on. Verify leads are returned to V/Ω — this is a pass/fail habit.

## Exercise 4 — key points

1. Warm, discolored receptacle; heater normal → heat is at the connection, not the load.
2. Loose/worn terminal or backstab connection adds resistance; at 12.5 A, even 0.1 Ω yields ~16 W of concentrated heat.
3. De-energize, replace receptacle, use screw terminals torqued to manufacturer spec (NEC 110.14(D)); consider a dedicated circuit.
4. LOTO, verify absence of voltage, repair, then thermal check under load by a qualified person.

## Grading

Pass: ≥ 80% on Exercise 2, safe meter practice in Exercise 3 (mandatory), reasonable explanation in 1 and 4.

### Part C — key points (⚠ mandatory)

- Resistance must be measured de-energized; the meter supplies its own test current.
- External 120 V on the Ω function corrupts the reading and can blow the meter fuse or destroy the meter.
- Correct procedure: switch off, unplug (or LOTO for hard-wired equipment), verify absence of voltage, isolate the element, then measure.
